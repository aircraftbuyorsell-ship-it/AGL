# EASA member states, the Czech Republic (OK-) and imports into Europe

General guidance for aircraft registered or to be registered in an EASA member state. Requirements vary by aircraft category and national authority. For a specific case, tell the user to confirm with the national aviation authority (in the Czech Republic, the Civil Aviation Authority, ÚCL), their CAMO or CAO, and a local aviation lawyer.

## Which rules apply

- EASA continuing airworthiness rules are in Regulation (EU) No 1321/2014.
- **Part-ML** applies to lighter, non-complex aircraft, including most piston general-aviation aeroplanes, under its weight and complexity limits. Many owners manage maintenance under a declared or approved aircraft maintenance programme (AMP).
- **Part-M** applies to larger or complex aircraft and to commercial operations; it usually requires a CAMO.
- Aircraft listed in Annex I to the EASA Basic Regulation (EU) 2018/1139, such as certain historic, amateur-built and ultralight aircraft, are outside EASA's scope and fall under national rules, which differ between countries.

Check which regime applies to the aircraft before applying any of the rest.

## Documents to verify

- Certificate of registration from the national register
- Certificate of airworthiness (CofA) or permit to fly
- Airworthiness Review Certificate (ARC) and its expiry date; the ARC is normally renewed every 12 months after an airworthiness review
- Aircraft maintenance programme (AMP) and evidence that maintenance followed it, including any approved deviations from the manufacturer's recommendations (for example engine operation beyond TBO on condition)
- Noise certificate, where applicable
- Radio station licence for the onboard radios
- Continuing airworthiness records: logbooks, AD status, modifications and repairs, component records including EASA Form 1 release certificates for installed parts

## Ownership, liens and transfer

- Confirm with the national register that the seller is the registered owner. In the Czech Republic the register is kept by ÚCL.
- How mortgages and liens over aircraft are recorded differs between countries, and some are recorded outside the aviation register. Recommend a local aviation lawyer to search for encumbrances.
- Larger airframes and engines may fall under the International Registry (Cape Town Convention), depending on the country and the size of the aircraft.
- A written purchase agreement and a trusted escrow arrangement apply as in any jurisdiction.
- VAT and customs duties may apply, especially to aircraft entering the EU from outside. Recommend a tax adviser before signing.

## Importing an aircraft into an EASA state (for example from the US)

Plan this before buying; some aircraft are expensive or impractical to import.

- **Type certificate:** the aircraft type and the specific model must be eligible for an EASA type certificate or validation.
- **Installed STCs and modifications:** each must be EASA-approved or validated. Unapproved modifications may need approval or removal. This is a common source of cost and delay.
- **Export airworthiness certificate** from the exporting authority (for the US, FAA Form 8130-4), then a CofA issued by the importing state's authority after its inspection.
- **ARC** issued after an airworthiness review on import.
- **Equipment requirements** that differ from the exporting country, such as 8.33 kHz VHF channel spacing radios, Mode S transponder requirements, ELT registration and radio licensing.
- **Deregistration** from the previous register, and evidence that no liens remain.
- **Customs and VAT** on import into the EU.
- **Records:** EASA authorities expect complete records; gaps may need resolving before a CofA is issued.

Recommend involving a CAMO or import specialist experienced with the type and the destination authority early, ideally before the pre-buy, so the inspection also checks import-critical items.

## Accident and incident history

Check the national safety investigation body for the registration and serial number (in the Czech Republic, the Air Accidents Investigation Institute, ÚZPLN), the previous country's databases for imported aircraft, and ask the seller for insurance claims history.
