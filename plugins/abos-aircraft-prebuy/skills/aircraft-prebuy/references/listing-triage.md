# Listing triage

A quick read of an advertised aircraft before anyone spends money on travel or an inspection. Triage judges only the information available. Its job is to decide whether the aircraft deserves the next step and which questions come first.

## How to triage

1. Extract the facts from the listing into a short table: make and model, year, serial number, registration, TT, engine SMOH or SFRM and the date of the last overhaul, propeller time and date, avionics, damage history statement, last annual or ARC date, location and price.
2. Mark every important field that is **missing**. A listing without a registration mark or serial number cannot be checked against a registry or AD database. Ask for both first.
3. Apply the criteria below and give one overall colour, with the reasons that set it.
4. List the top five questions to resolve, the first of which should usually be "send the registration, serial numbers and full logbook scans".

## Green: worth pursuing

All of the following hold, as far as the listing shows:

- Registration and serial number are given, and the seller says they are the registered owner or their authorised broker
- Complete logbooks back to new are stated to be available
- No damage history, or damage history disclosed with documented repairs (Form 337s or equivalent repair records)
- Engine time and calendar age leave meaningful life before the manufacturer's recommended overhaul, or the price clearly reflects a run-out engine
- Annual inspection (FAA) or ARC (EASA) is current or recent
- Seller agrees to an independent pre-buy inspection at a shop of the buyer's choice
- Price is within the range of comparable listings the user can find (do not supply the range yourself)

## Amber: proceed with care

Any of these:

- Logbook gaps, reconstructed logs, or "logs available on request" with no scans offered
- Disclosed damage history, even if repaired
- Engine past the recommended TBO in hours or calendar years, run "on condition", or with a top overhaul history the listing does not explain
- Long periods of storage or very low hours over many years (corrosion and internal engine corrosion risk)
- Major modifications (engine conversions, STC'd airframe changes) without stated paperwork
- Aircraft located in a coastal, humid or flood-prone area, or kept outside
- Recent change of ownership followed quickly by resale
- Unusual history: ex-training, ex-agricultural, ex-skydiving, ex-glider-tug, ex-pipeline or other high-cycle or high-stress use
- Import or export involved, or registration in a country the buyer is unfamiliar with
- Inspection status unclear, or "fresh annual" done by a shop connected to the seller

## Red: stop or resolve first

Any of these:

- Seller refuses an independent pre-buy inspection or refuses to share logbook scans
- No logbooks, or missing engine logbooks
- Seller is not the registered owner and cannot prove authority to sell
- Pressure to pay a deposit or the full price before inspection, especially by wire to a personal account, cryptocurrency or gift cards
- Price far below comparable aircraft without a clear, documented reason
- Contact details, photos or text that appear copied from another listing
- Known prop strike or sudden engine stoppage without a documented teardown inspection
- Substantial damage with no repair documentation
- Unresolved liens or legal disputes over ownership

See `red-flags.md` for detail on fraud patterns.
