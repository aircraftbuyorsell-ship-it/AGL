# FAA (N-registered) aircraft and US transactions

General guidance for aircraft on the US register. Regulations change, so for any specific requirement tell the user to confirm the current text in the electronic Code of Federal Regulations (ecfr.gov) and on the FAA website.

## Verify ownership and registration

- Look up the registration mark in the FAA Aircraft Registry inquiry (registry.faa.gov) and confirm the registered owner, serial number, make and model match the listing and the data plate.
- Check that the registration is valid and not expired, pending or revoked.
- If the seller is not the registered owner, ask for the documents that give them authority to sell: chain of title, power of attorney, or a broker agreement.

## Title and liens

- Use an aviation title company to search the FAA aircraft record for recorded liens, security interests and gaps in the chain of title, and to provide title insurance if wanted.
- Larger aircraft and engines may also have interests registered in the International Registry under the Cape Town Convention. A title company can say whether the airframe or engines are large enough to fall under it and search accordingly.
- Liens must be released before or at closing, and the releases recorded.

## Transfer documents

- The FAA bill of sale (AC Form 8050-2) or an equivalent document conveying ownership
- The aircraft registration application (AC Form 8050-1) filed by the buyer
- The buyer can operate the aircraft for a limited period on the pink copy of the registration application while the new certificate is issued, within the conditions the FAA sets; confirm the current rules, including restrictions on international flights
- A written purchase agreement covering price, deposit, the pre-buy inspection, handling of discrepancies, delivery location, risk of loss before closing and closing conditions
- An aviation escrow agent holds funds and documents and releases them when both parties' conditions are met

## Airworthiness and records rules

- The aircraft needs a valid airworthiness certificate (standard or special) and a current registration on board.
- Annual inspection every 12 calendar months; a 100-hour inspection is also required if the aircraft is used for hire or flight instruction for hire.
- Maintenance records must show the current status of applicable ADs, life-limited parts, time since overhaul of items that are overhauled on a time basis, the current inspection status, and records of major alterations. When records are missing, AD and inspection status has to be re-established, which is costly and reduces value.
- Major repairs and alterations are documented on FAA Form 337, with a copy kept by the FAA. The buyer can request the aircraft's records file from the FAA, which includes 337s on file.
- For Part 91 non-commercial operations, the manufacturer's recommended TBO is generally not mandatory, but it strongly affects value and insurance. For commercial operations it can be required.
- ADS-B Out is required in much of US controlled airspace. Check the installed equipment and its compliance.

## Accident and incident history

- NTSB CAROL database: search by registration mark and serial number
- FAA accident and incident data
- Insurance claims history: ask the seller directly

## Taxes and costs

Sales and use tax depends on the state and on where the aircraft is delivered and based. Recommend a tax adviser before choosing the closing location. Do not give a tax figure.

## Export from the US

If the aircraft will leave the US register:

- The buyer's authority will normally require an export certificate of airworthiness (FAA Form 8130-4), issued after an inspection
- Cancellation of the US registration, and confirmation that no liens remain
- Check the importing country's requirements before buying, especially for installed STCs; see `jurisdiction-easa.md` for EASA imports
