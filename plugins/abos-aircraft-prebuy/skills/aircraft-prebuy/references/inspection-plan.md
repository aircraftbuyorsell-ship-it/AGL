# Pre-buy inspection plan

A pre-buy inspection is the buyer's independent look at the aircraft and its records before committing. It is not automatically an annual inspection or an airworthiness review. The buyer and seller should agree in writing what it covers.

## Set-up

- **Independent inspector.** Use a shop or licensed inspector chosen by the buyer, with no business relationship to the seller or the seller's broker. Experience on the specific type matters more than convenience.
- **Written scope and price** agreed before the aircraft goes in, including what the inspector may open, remove or test.
- **Seller consent** for ground time, panel removal, test flights and any engine runs.
- **Discrepancy handling.** Before the inspection, agree in the purchase agreement who pays to correct airworthiness discrepancies and how cosmetic or non-airworthiness items are handled.
- **Records first.** Have the inspector, or someone qualified, review the logbooks before or during the physical inspection; findings in the records shape what to open.
- **Buyer present** if possible, at least for the debrief.
- **Pre-buy or annual.** For piston aircraft, buyers often combine the pre-buy with an annual inspection done by their own inspector, so the aircraft changes hands with a fresh, independent annual. Discuss this option.

## Core scope for all aircraft

- Complete records review (see `logbook-review.md`)
- Verification that data plates and serial numbers of airframe, engine and propeller match the records and registration
- Corrosion inspection of primary structure, including spar areas, wing attach fittings, control surfaces, tail, belly and areas under the floor, using inspection panels
- Flight controls, cables, pulleys and travel stops
- Landing gear, brakes and attach points; signs of hard landings or gear-up damage
- Firewall, engine mounts and exhaust system
- Fuel system: tanks or bladders, leaks, sumps, selector and caps
- Electrical system and wiring condition
- Installed equipment works and matches the equipment list and weight and balance
- Avionics function check, including transponder and, where required, ADS-B Out compliance
- Evidence of repairs that do not appear in the records: mismatched paint, non-standard rivets, skin waviness, filler
- Test flight with a qualified pilot, checking engine performance against book values, systems, trim, rigging and avionics

## Piston engines

- Cylinder differential compression test, recorded per cylinder, done warm
- Borescope of every cylinder: valves, cylinder walls and piston crowns
- Oil filter or screen inspection for metal
- Oil sample for laboratory analysis; most useful when compared with earlier results
- Magneto check and timing, ignition harness
- Induction and exhaust leak checks; turbocharger and wastegate where fitted
- Engine monitor data download if a monitor is installed (EGT and CHT patterns, prior over-temperatures)
- Signs of long-term inactivity: internal corrosion risk, especially on the camshaft and lifters of engines that sat unused

## Propellers

- Blade condition, nicks, prior dressing and track
- Hub, spinner and bulkhead cracks
- Constant-speed operation, governor function and oil leaks
- Calendar and hour status against the manufacturer's recommendation

## Twins

Everything above for each engine and propeller, plus:

- Single-engine performance and handling on the test flight, as appropriate
- Feathering and unfeathering function
- Cross-feed fuel system
- Matching of engine times and the overhaul plan for both engines

## Turboprops and light jets

The scope is larger and should be planned with a service centre experienced on the type:

- Engine trend monitoring data review and borescope of the hot section
- Compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance programme and inspection intervals (phase or calendar inspections)
- Status and transferability of engine, airframe and avionics maintenance programmes
- Pressurisation system and cabin leak check
- Ice protection systems
- RVSM approval and compliance, where relevant
- Records of components with life limits or overhaul times
- Corrosion on high-cycle or coastal-based aircraft
- Damage history checks, including bird strikes and lightning strikes

## Helicopters

- Life-limited dynamic components (main and tail rotor, transmissions, drive shafts) are critical; check each component's time and remaining life against the records
- Rotor track and balance records and vibration levels
- Gearbox chip detectors and oil analysis
- Calendar limits on components and elastomeric parts

## After the inspection

- Get a written report with findings sorted into airworthiness items and other observations, with photographs.
- Use the findings to decide: proceed, proceed with corrections at the seller's cost, renegotiate, or walk away.
- Keep the report: it is a useful baseline for future maintenance.
